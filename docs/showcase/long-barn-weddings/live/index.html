Section one · the weekend

Forty-five hours, in order.

Friday 2pm to Sunday 11am. This is the whole thing, hour by hour, with nothing quietly left off it.

Friday · 2pm

The keys, and an empty barn

Friday 2pm: the keys, the barn empty and swept, and eight hours to make it yours. Nobody stands over you with a schedule. The caretaker shows you the fuse board, the water, and the switch nobody can ever find, then leaves you to it.

Friday · 9pm

Pizza on the floor

Most couples eat pizza on the floor at nine and it is usually the best bit of the weekend. Half the table has driven three hours. Nobody is dressed. Somebody has already put the lights up twice.

Saturday · 1pm

Ceremony, inside or in the meadow

Saturday: ceremony inside or in the meadow, drinks in the yard, long tables the length of the barn, and a licence until 1am with amplified music until midnight.

The meadow — ceremony at one, if the sky allows

Saturday · 3pm

Drinks in the yard

Cobbles, a low wall everyone ends up sitting on, and twelve acres for people to wander into. If the weather turns, the barn doors are eight feet wide and the whole thing moves inside in about four minutes.

Saturday · 6pm

Long tables, the length of the barn

Fourteen trestles end to end under the trusses. Your caterer serves from the yard end and the band builds at the other, so nothing has to be shifted between courses.

Saturday · midnight

Amplified music off, licence until one

The band stops at twelve and the bar runs to one. The last hour is nearly always the quiet one — in the yard, doors open, somebody making tea for eleven people.

Sunday · 9am

Breakfast in the farmhouse

Sunday: breakfast in the farmhouse, a slow clear-up, out by eleven. We do not chase you at nine with a clipboard.

The farmhouse — nine bedrooms, twenty-two beds

Sunday · 11am

Out by eleven

Bins are ours, bottles are yours, and the field will still be there. Most people leave later than they planned and we have never once minded.

Section two · the building

Thirty-four metres of barn, and what it does in the rain.

Barn is 34m by 11m. 140 seated at long tables, 96 with a dance floor and a band, 180 standing. Fully covered, heated, and it has never once been rained off.

In the rain

Nothing dramatic happens. Two sets of doors close, the heaters go on, the ceremony moves under the trusses, and the light gets better. In eleven years we have moved fourteen ceremonies indoors at short notice, and not one couple has said afterwards that it spoiled the day.

The unglamorous facts

The floor is flagstone and oak, level throughout, and it takes a heel. Heating is underfloor at the yard end and three radiant panels at the other. There is a step at the west door and a ramp beside it. The nearest neighbour is 400 metres away, which is why the licence runs to one.
