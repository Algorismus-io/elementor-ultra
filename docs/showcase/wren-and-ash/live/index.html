How a commission works

Four stages, and you are welcome at all of them.

01

We visit and measure

We come to the room the piece will live in, measure it, and look at the light. No charge and no obligation — it is the only way to get proportion right.

02

A drawing and a fixed price

A hand drawing at a proper scale, with a fixed price against it. Nothing moves until you are happy with both. The price does not change afterwards.

03

Timber selection

We pick the boards out of the rack and lay them out. You are welcome to attend and most people do — it is the part where the piece stops being a drawing.

04

Twelve to twenty weeks, then delivery

The two of us in the workshop, no subcontracting. We deliver and install it ourselves, in our own van, and take the packing away with us.

Detail plates: the joints, and the room they were cut in.

Plates i & ii

Plate i — through-dovetail, oak

Cut by hand, sawn to the line and pared, no router jig. Finished in hardwax oil, not lacquer. It will mark, and you can fix it yourself with a cloth in ten minutes. That is the trade and we think it is the right one.

Plate ii — the bench room

Two benches, one bandsaw, a rack of moulding planes that came with the building. Every piece in the catalogue above was made standing at one of these two benches.

The wait, and why it is that long

The current wait is about seven months. We will not take more than nine commissions a year, because ten would mean hiring someone and then it would not be our work.

If that is too long, we would rather say so now than rush a piece. We will tell you the true date at the drawing stage and we have not missed one yet.

Start a conversation about a room.

Tell us the room, roughly what it has to do, and when you would like it. We will answer within the week, and if we are not the right workshop for it we will say who is.
