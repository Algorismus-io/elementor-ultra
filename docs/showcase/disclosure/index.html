About this content

Everything in the exjsx demo archive is fictional. None of these businesses exists.

If you have arrived here from one of the fifty demo sites — the menopause clinic, the valve works, the refugee charity, the CPAP machine, the children's dentist, any of them — that business is not real. It has no premises, no staff, no products, no customers and no accreditations. It was invented by AI software about a day before you saw it.

Why these pages exist

exjsx is an open-source toolchain that lets AI coding agents build native Elementor websites. To show what it can do, we asked AI agents to invent fifty businesses across fifty industries and build a complete landing page for each one, end to end, in a single working day. The point of the exercise was the design and build capability — can an agent produce a page that looks like a real designer made it? — not the businesses themselves, which are set dressing.

These are demonstration builds, not case studies. No client commissioned any of them. No one was paid for any of them. Nothing in them describes real work for a real customer.

What specifically is made up

All of it. To be exact, across the fifty sites the following are entirely fabricated:

  • The businesses and brands. All fifty names were generated by AI. They were not checked against any trade mark or company register. Some of them turn out to coincide with the names of real businesses. See "If your name is here" below.
  • The people. Every named person is invented — the doctors, the dentists, the solicitors, the reporters, the conference speakers, the engineers, the founders. Their photographs are AI-generated images of people who do not exist.
  • The qualifications. Several pages show medical, dental and legal credentials — MBBS, MRCGP, MRCOG, British Menopause Society certificates, postgraduate paediatric dental qualifications, and so on. No one holds these, because no one in these pages is a person. The awarding bodies named are real; the awards are not.
  • The registrations. Where a page shows a company number, a charity number, a licence number or a claim to be registered with a regulator such as the CQC or the GMC, that is invented text. It is not a real registration and, where it resembles one, we have replaced it — see "Corrections" below.
  • The certifications and approvals. API 6D, ISO standards, ATEX, Lloyd's Register type approval, PED, MCS, TÜV, FDA 510(k) clearance, UKCA marking, NVQ and AM2 qualifications, NHS contracts. None of these were granted to anything. The standards bodies are real organisations that have never heard of these fictional companies.
  • The reviews and testimonials. Every quotation attributed to a customer, patient, client or employee is invented. Nobody said any of it.
  • The numbers. Every price, statistic, success rate, performance figure, test result and outcome claim is invented. Several are quoted to a precision — decibel readings to one decimal place, appeal success rates to the percentage point — that makes them look measured. They were not measured. They were written to look plausible, which is exactly what makes them worth warning you about.
  • The contact details. Phone numbers, email addresses, postal addresses and domains are part of the fiction. Do not use them.

Nothing here is advice or an offer

Nothing in the demo archive is medical, clinical, legal, financial, tax or any other professional advice. Nothing in it is an offer to sell or supply anything, or an invitation to do business.

Several demo sites depict regulated activities — private healthcare, a medical device, legal representation, immigration advice, payroll and tax services, a carbon credit registry, children's education. The fictional businesses hold none of the authorisations those activities require.

Nothing in this archive has been cleared, approved, registered, licensed or certified by anyone. In the United States, nothing here has been reviewed by the Food and Drug Administration — the "FDA 510(k) cleared" line on the sleep-apnoea demo is invented text about a device that does not exist — or by the SEC, the IRS, or any state medical, dental or bar licensing authority. In the United Kingdom, nothing here is registered with or known to the CQC, the GMC, the GDC, the SRA, the FCA, the MHRA, the OISC, Ofsted or the Charity Commission. The standards bodies, awarding bodies and certification schemes named across these pages are real organisations that have never heard of any of these companies.

If you need real help with any of these things, please go to a real, regulated provider. In the US, start with your own physician, your state bar's lawyer referral service, or the SEC's Investor.gov. In the UK, start with your GP, the Law Society's Find a Solicitor, or the FCA Register.

How the content was made

  • Text: written by AI agents (large language models) from a one-paragraph brief per site. No human wrote or fact-checked the copy.
  • Design and code: generated by AI agents using exjsx, and built into native Elementor pages.
  • Images: 112 images generated with FLUX.1 [schnell] (Black Forest Labs) via the fal.ai API. Everyone depicted is a synthetic person who does not exist.
  • Human involvement: setting up the run, writing the briefs, and reviewing the output afterwards. The content itself is machine-generated.

Each demo page carries a permanent notice saying so, in the page itself and in its metadata.

If your name is here

Some of the fifty invented names coincide with real businesses. We know of several already and have listed them at https://exjsx.dev/showcase/name-collisions/. There may be more we have not found.

If you are, or act for, a real business, charity or person whose name, mark, address, registration number, telephone number or likeness appears anywhere in this archive, email legal@wpos.ai.

We will acknowledge within 2 working days and remove or rename within 5 working days. We will not ask you to prove your rights first and we will not argue about it. These are demonstration pages with no commercial value to us and no reason to exist at your expense.

Corrections

We maintain a public log of changes made to the archive at https://exjsx.dev/showcase/changelog/, including every name changed, every fabricated registration number removed, and every takedown actioned.

Corrections we have identified and scheduled before launch — the changelog records each one when it lands:

  • Invented company registration numbers, a charity number, and telephone numbers that turned out to sit in real allocated ranges, to be replaced with reserved, unusable equivalents.
  • Six business names that collide with real trading entities, several in the same industry, to be renamed.
  • Contact addresses at three domains registered to real third parties, and one real residential postcode, to be replaced.
  • References to real third-party organisations as clients, employers or integration partners, to be removed.

We have listed them as scheduled rather than done because, at the time of writing, they are scheduled rather than done.
