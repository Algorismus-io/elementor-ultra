03 — The four trades

What the work is actually like

Not the brochure version. Four trades, what you will physically be doing in year one, and what the ceiling looks like once you are qualified.

01

Electrical

Mostly domestic and commercial installation, some fault finding. You will be on your knees in lofts for two years and running your own board by year four.

Time to qualify

4 years to NVQ3 + AM2

Qualified pay

£38,000 – £46,000

Getting in

Competitive — apply early

02

Plumbing & heating

Domestic heating, bathrooms and commercial pipework. Emergency callouts pay a premium, and once you are out on your own around year six that rate is yours.

Time to qualify

3 to 4 years to NVQ3

Qualified pay

£34,000 – £44,000

Getting in

Steady intake all year

03

HVAC & refrigeration

Air conditioning, refrigeration and heat pumps. The newest of the four and the fastest growing. F-Gas certification is the ticket, and every new building needs one of you.

Time to qualify

4 years + F-Gas

Qualified pay

£36,000 – £48,000

Getting in

Easiest to get started

04

Welding & fabrication

Fabrication, pipework and structural. Hardest to get into, best paid at the top, and coded welders can name their rate offshore and in petrochem.

Time to qualify

3 years + coding tickets

Qualified pay

£38,000 – £70,000 coded

Getting in

Hardest — test before offer

05 — Entry requirements

Honestly stated

Most employers want Maths and English at grade 4, a driving licence by year two, and someone who turns up at 7:20 for a 7:30 start. That last one is genuinely the main filter.

01

Maths and English at grade 4

Most employers want the two of them. A few will take a functional skills pass instead, and a handful will put you through it in year one if the rest of you is right.

02

A driving licence by year two

Not on day one. Vans go out at 7:30 and a mate who can drive one is worth more than a mate who cannot, so employers expect you to be working on it.

03

Someone who turns up at 7:20 for a 7:30 start

That last one is genuinely the main filter. More apprenticeships end over timekeeping than over ability, and every employer we place with says the same thing unprompted.

04

No GCSEs at all? Still worth applying

Sixty-one of our employers run their own entry test and ignore the paperwork entirely. We flag those on your matches so you are not applying into a wall.

07 — For parents

The questions you are about to ask

Is it a real qualification?

Yes it is a real qualification. An NVQ Level 3 with AM2 is the same standard the person who wired your house holds, it is regulated, and it is recognised by every employer in the sector.

Can they go to university later?

Yes they can go to university later and many do. A Level 3 carries UCAS points, and the ones who go tend to go at 24 with savings, a trade to fall back on and a much clearer idea of why.

Is the trade disappearing?

No, the trade is not disappearing. There are 11,000 unfilled electrical vacancies right now, and a heat pump is not a thing you install from an office in another country.

What if they pick the wrong one?

The first two years overlap more than you would think. Moving between trades before the end of year two is common, costs nothing, and we handle the rematch rather than leaving them to it.
