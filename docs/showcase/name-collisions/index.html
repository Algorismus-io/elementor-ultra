Names in this archive that collide with real businesses

The fifty businesses in the exjsx demo archive were invented by AI. The names were generated the same way, and none of them was cleared against any trade mark register or company register before the sites were built. That was a mistake in how the exercise was run, and this page is part of correcting it.

We went back afterwards and looked for real businesses trading under each of the fifty names. Twenty-six of the fifty collide with something real. They are all listed below.

If your business is on this page, or should be and is not: email legal@wpos.ai. We will acknowledge within 2 working days and rename or remove within 5. We will not ask you to prove your rights first and we will not argue about it. These are demonstration pages with no commercial value to us and no reason at all to exist at your expense.

An important limitation

No official trade mark register was successfully searched. The UK IPO register is behind a form we could not query, and the EUIPO and USPTO interfaces refused automated access. Every entry below rests on evidence that a real business trades under the name — a live website, a company or charity record — and not on evidence of a registered trade mark. A registered mark we did not find would make a collision more serious, not less. A proper clearance search is commissioned and this page will be updated when it reports.

So: this list is what we found by looking. It is not a legal opinion, and it is certainly not a guarantee that the other twenty-four names are clear.

Being renamed — six

These six are exact-name collisions with real, trading businesses, several of them in the same industry as the demo. A disclaimer does not fix this, because the name travels without the page: into a search result, a screenshot, a link pasted into a chat. All six are being renamed before launch, and the demo pages keep their existing web addresses so that links do not break.

Domains and registration numbers

Three demo pages used contact addresses at domains registered to real third parties, and three used company or charity registration numbers that turn out to be real allocations rather than invented ones. Those are being replaced with reserved, unusable equivalents. The changelog records each one as it lands.
